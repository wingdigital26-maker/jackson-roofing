Crew Shot List

Snap these on every job. Two minutes of photos turns one roof into a week of social posts. Shoot in daylight, hold the phone level, get close on the damage.

Daily minimum: 1 damage close-up 1 in-progress 1 before + after pair 1 short video clip

1 Damage close-ups

These stop the scroll and teach homeowners what to look for.

  • Hail bruise or dent on a shinglePut a coin or chalk next to it for scale.
  • Cracked or lifted shingles, exposed nail heads
  • Granules piled in the gutter or at the downspoutLooks like coarse black sand.
  • Soft or rotted decking board pulled off
  • Flashing pulling away from a chimney or vent
  • Interior: water stain on a rafter or ceilingThe "why it matters" shot.

2 Work in progress

Real work on a real roof is what earns trust.

  • Tear-off in actionOld shingles coming off, debris to the dumpster.
  • Fresh underlayment going down
  • Shingle bundles staged on the roof
  • A clean snapped chalk line before the first course
  • Hands working a course / nail gun
  • Magnet sweep of the yard at the endThe "we clean up after ourselves" shot people love.

3 Before & after

Your #1 performer. Always shoot the BEFORE before you start, same angle for both.

  • Whole-house curb appeal: old roof vs new roof
  • One tight detail pairA valley, a ridge, or a chimney.

4 People & trust

Roofing is bought on trust. Faces and the truck sell.

  • Crew on the roof, wide shot
  • An inspection in progress, clipboard in hand
  • The branded truck at the job
  • Keys-back / handshake with a happy homeownerAsk permission first.
  • Yard sign going in

5 Aerial & drone

Premium and very shareable when you have the drone up.

  • Overhead of the finished roof
  • Slow orbit of the completed home

6 Video clips (10 to 30 seconds)

Short video grows a following faster than photos. Just hold steady and film.

  • "Spot hail damage" — point at 3 things on a shingle
  • Tear-off or full-roof time-lapseSet the phone on something and let it run.
  • Underlayment install (oddly satisfying)
  • Driveway magnet nail-sweep
  • 10-second meet-the-crew intro
  • Drone flyover of the finished roof
  • Storm day: "if your street got hit, here's step one"

✓ Five rules that make the pics land

  • Shoot in daylight. Bad light kills a good roof.
  • Get close on damage. Fill the frame.
  • Always grab the BEFORE before you touch the roof.
  • Hold the phone level and steady. Two seconds of stillness beats a shaky clip.
  • Get a quick verbal OK before showing a homeowner or their house.
