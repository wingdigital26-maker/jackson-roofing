Film & Post

The whole social plan runs on one habit: shoot a little on every job, post it consistently. Phone only, no gear, no agency. This is how the pros who actually get roofing leads do it.

1. Capture2. Film3. Post4. Repeat

1 The weekly minimum

Consistency beats everything. Roofers who post once a month get nothing. This is the floor, not the ceiling.

  • 3 short vertical videos a week. 15 to 30 seconds, shot on the job. One is plenty per day you are out.
  • A before + after on every job. Same camera angle both times. This is the highest-converting content there is.
  • Post the finished job to Google Business Profile. A few photos + a line of text. This is what feeds the Google map and books local calls.
  • Ask every happy customer for a review, and grab a 20-second phone testimonial when they will give one.

2 How to film it (phone only)

Raw phone footage of the owner or crew beats polished agency video. People trust real.

  • Hold the phone vertical. That is how Reels, Shorts, TikTok and stories play.
  • Hook them in the first 2 seconds. Say the interesting thing first, do not warm up. The opening line is 80% of whether anyone watches.
  • Daylight, hold steady 2 to 3 seconds per shot. Get close on the damage.
  • Talk to the camera like a neighbor. Owner or crew on camera cuts the cost of every lead. A face sells, a logo does not.
  • Assume the sound is off. Most people watch muted, so the visual has to carry it and captions get added later.
  • 15 to 30 seconds. Shorter and clearer wins.

3 The video types that actually work

Shoot these on repeat. Every one is proven to perform for roofers.

Before & after

Same angle, storm-worn then finished. The #1 performer. Shoot the BEFORE before you touch the roof.

Adjuster vs. roofer

Point the camera at the hail hits the insurance adjuster "did not see." This hail-claim genre is the most viral roofing content there is.

Tear-off time-lapse

Phone on a tripod, whole roof in a day sped to 30 seconds. Looks impressive, shows you move fast.

"How we found the leak"

Walk through spotting a leak three other companies missed. How-to content gets far above-average engagement.

Quick FAQ

Answer one question in 20 seconds: repair or replace, will my yard be a mess, how long does a claim take.

Homeowner testimonial

A real Plano customer on their finished roof, in their own words. Let the customer do the selling.

4 Hooks that stop the scroll

The first line. Steal these openers and fill in the job.

  • "This Plano roof passed a free inspection last week. Watch what we found."
  • "The adjuster said no damage. Then I pointed the camera here."
  • "Repair or replace? Here is the 10-second test."
  • "Watch this roof go from storm-wrecked to done in 30 seconds."
  • "Your insurance has a deadline you do not know about."
  • "5 things your roofer hopes you never ask."
  • "How we found a leak three other companies missed."
  • "If your neighbor got a new roof after the last hail, read this."

5 Where to post (in priority order)

Spend your time where local jobs actually come from. Not all platforms are equal for a roofer.

Tier 1
Google Business Profile. Project photos + a short post + relentless review requests. This feeds the map pack, the top source of local roofing calls.
Tier 2
One short video, 3x a week, posted to Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and TikTok at once. Film once, post three places. Pin your best before/after and one hail-claim explainer.
Tier 3
Nextdoor. Keep the page alive, then go hard in the days right after any North Texas hail or wind event. Neighbor trust books jobs.
Home base
Facebook. Your community and review hub. Post the same content here, join local groups, but do not pour money into cheap lead-form ads.
Skip
Chasing TikTok follower counts, daily logo graphics, over-produced video, and paid Facebook lead forms (about 99% of those leads are not serious).

6 Two rules that tie it together

  • 80/20. Four of every five posts should teach or build trust. Only one in five is a straight pitch.
  • Geo-anchor everything. Name the neighborhood or city (Plano, Frisco, McKinney, Wylie). Local proof beats reach every time for booking jobs.
  • Give it 30 to 60 days. Consistent posting turns into inbound calls in a month or two, not overnight. Do not quit at week two.
The one habit: shoot 3 short clips a week on the job and post them. Everything else is detail. The captions are already written and waiting in the plan, so all the crew has to do is capture.
