# Jackson Roofing: Social Media Playbook

A professional social presence for a family-run North Texas roofer. This is a
Wing-owned, draft-only asset (Chris Jackson is not an active client). Nothing in
here posts to a live account. It documents what a pro roofing social manager
would actually run, and it drives the local drafting engine.

**Business facts we can stand on:** family-run Plano roofer, serving North Texas
since 2000. Storm and hail specialty. Service area: Plano, Allen, McKinney,
Frisco, Wylie and the surrounding North Dallas suburbs.

**Voice:** plain, local, trustworthy. Talk like a neighbor who has been on North
Texas roofs for two decades, not a brochure. No hype, no fake urgency, no made-up
numbers. No phone-number calls to action anywhere; always point to "Contact Us"
or the site. No em dashes.

---

## Why social matters for a roofer

Roofing is a trust-and-timing business. Nobody wakes up wanting a new roof; they
need one after a storm, or when they are selling, or when a neighbor's job looks
good. A steady, honest social presence does three jobs:

1. **Proof.** Photos of real work and real reviews answer the only question a
   homeowner has: can I trust these people on my house.
2. **Recall.** When hail hits, the roofer people already follow is the one they
   message. Consistency now pays off on the one day demand spikes.
3. **Local SEO signal.** An active Google Business Profile with fresh posts,
   photos and reviews lifts the map pack, which is where roofing leads actually
   come from.

## Content pillars

Every draft maps to one pillar. A healthy week touches most of them.

| Key | Pillar | What it is and why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `storm` | Storm & hail response | Timely posts around North Texas hail and wind events. Highest intent, most shareable. What to do after a storm, inspection reminders, insurance-claim basics. |
| `beforeafter` | Before & after jobs | Real project photos, the problem and the fix. The single most persuasive content a roofer has. |
| `trust` | Crew & family since 2000 | The people, the workmanship, two decades in the same community. Builds the relationship that closes jobs. |
| `education` | Homeowner education | Practical, no-jargon answers: signs of hail damage, when a repair beats a replacement, ventilation, gutters, roof age. Positions the brand as the expert. |
| `community` | Local & community | Plano, Allen, McKinney, Frisco, Wylie neighborhoods, seasons, local events. Signals a real local business, not a storm-chaser. |
| `reviews` | Reviews & social proof | Homeowner quotes and ratings, always with permission and never fabricated. |
| `seasonal` | Seasonal & maintenance | Spring storm prep, summer heat and ventilation, fall gutter and flashing checks, winter inspections. Ties the calendar to a reason to act. |

**Truth guardrail:** never invent review counts, job totals, response times or
star ratings. If a real figure is not on hand, speak to the work itself, not a
number.

## Platforms: what a pro posts and why

### Google Business Profile (GBP)
The highest-ROI surface for a local roofer. This is where map-pack ranking and
"roofer near me" intent live.
- **Post:** completed jobs with photos, storm-response updates, seasonal
  reminders, review highlights. Keep GBP posts short and action-light.
- **Why:** fresh posts and photos are a direct local-ranking signal, and the
  profile is the first thing a searching homeowner sees.
- **Cadence:** 2 to 3 posts per week. Add photos to the profile with every job.
- **CTA:** "Learn more" pointing to the site. No phone numbers in copy.

### Facebook
Where North Texas homeowners, neighborhood groups and word-of-mouth live.
- **Post:** before-and-afters, crew and family story, community posts, education,
  reviews. Longer captions are fine; Facebook rewards a real story.
- **Why:** the sharing and local-group culture turns one happy customer into
  neighborhood reach. Strongest recall channel between storms.
- **Cadence:** 3 to 4 posts per week.
- **CTA:** "Contact Us" via the site link.

### Instagram
The visual portfolio. Roofing is inherently photogenic; use it.
- **Post:** clean before-and-after carousels, drone or ridge shots, short
  process clips, crew moments. Caption is secondary to the image.
- **Why:** builds the credibility a homeowner checks before reaching out, and
  reaches younger homeowners and realtors.
- **Cadence:** 2 to 3 posts per week. Prioritize image quality over volume.
- **CTA:** link in bio to the site.

### Nextdoor
The most local, most trust-driven surface. Neighbors asking neighbors for a
roofer.
- **Post:** genuinely helpful, low-key updates. Storm-response help, seasonal
  reminders, "we work in your neighborhood" notes. Never salesy; Nextdoor
  punishes hard-sell.
- **Why:** recommendations here carry more weight than any ad. This is where
  "who did your roof" threads happen.
- **Cadence:** 1 to 2 posts per week, plus organic replies.
- **CTA:** soft. "Contact Us" via the site, no pressure.

## Geo strategy

Lead with the specific city, not "North Texas," whenever a job or post is tied to
a place. Rotate coverage so no city goes dark:

- **Core five:** Plano, Allen, McKinney, Frisco, Wylie.
- Name the neighborhood or ISD when you can ("worked a hail claim off Legacy in
  Plano this week").
- On GBP, the service-area cities reinforce the profile's geo relevance.
- One post should read as clearly local to one place, not a list of every city.

  dominates the feed.

## Best time to post

North Texas is Central time (America/Chicago). These are practical local-service
norms for a home-services audience, offered as guidance, not measured data. Watch
the profile's own insights once real posts accrue and let that override this.

| Platform | Best windows (CT) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google Business Profile | Weekday mornings, 8 to 11 a.m. | Aligns with "roofer near me" search intent as people start the day. Freshness matters more than exact minute. |
| Facebook | Weekday midday 11 a.m. to 1 p.m., and early evening 6 to 8 p.m. | Lunch scroll and after-dinner scroll. Community and story posts do best in the evening slot. |
| Instagram | Late morning 10 a.m. to 12 p.m., weekday evenings 5 to 7 p.m. | Visual posts catch the mid-morning and after-work scroll. Weekend late morning also works for community shots. |
| Nextdoor | Weekday mornings 8 to 10 a.m., Sunday mornings | Neighbors check in with coffee. Helpful, low-key notes land best early in the day. |

Rules of thumb: post storm content the moment it is relevant, not at the "ideal"
hour. Never sacrifice a timely storm post to wait for a posting window. And
consistency of days beats chasing the perfect minute.

| Thu | Instagram | Education or process |
| Thu | Nextdoor | Community / helpful note |

The engine rotates pillars over a 4-week cycle so the feed never repeats itself
and every pillar gets airtime across the month.

## Storm-response playbook

When a real North Texas hail or wind event hits, the calendar pauses and this
takes over. This is the highest-intent window a roofer gets, and it closes fast.

**Within 0 to 24 hours**
- Post to GBP first: short, calm, "we are inspecting roofs in [affected city]
  this week, free and no pressure." GBP is where the "roofer near me" searches
  spike immediately after a storm.
- Post to Facebook: a neighborly check-in naming the affected areas, an offer of
  a free inspection, and one genuinely useful safety or next-step note. Invite
  comments about which streets got hit hardest.
- Post to Nextdoor: purely helpful. What to look for, why documenting early
  matters, and that you are a local family-run roofer glad to take a look. No
  hard sell; Nextdoor punishes it.

**Within 24 to 48 hours**
- Instagram: real photos of hail bruising or wind-lifted shingles from local
  inspections, with a plain-language "here is what damage actually looks like."
- Facebook follow-up: insurance-claim basics done honestly. Damage is often
  invisible from the ground, insurers want it documented soon after the event,
  and a free inspection gives photos either way.
- Keep GBP fresh with a second post if inspections are filling up.

**Ground rules during a storm push**
- Speak to the specific affected cities, not "North Texas" in general.
- Never invent damage, urgency, or claim outcomes. "A free inspection tells you
  either way" is the honest frame.
- Lead with being local and family-run since 2000. The contrast with out-of-town
  storm-chasers is the whole advantage, so make it plainly.
- Still no phone numbers in copy. Contact Us via the site.
- Pull back to the normal calendar within a week as intent settles.

## What "good" looks like

- Every post ties to a real place, a real job type, or a real season. No filler.
- Photos on almost everything; a roofer's feed without pictures is a wasted feed.
- Reviews and numbers are real or not stated.
- CTAs send people to the site to Contact Us, never to a phone number in copy.
- Consistency beats bursts: a steady 8 to 10 posts a week across platforms holds
  recall far better than a flood followed by silence.
