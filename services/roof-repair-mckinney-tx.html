We keep the National Weather Service storm reports for our service area because they settle arguments. Between June 2, 2024 and May 24, 2026 there were 2 confirmed hail reports logged in McKinney itself, across 2 separate storm days. The largest measured 1.25 inches, about half dollar sized.

Why this matters for your roof: hail around one inch is usually enough to bruise asphalt shingles and knock the protective granules loose, and that damage often does not leak for months or years. If your McKinney roof was under one of the storms above and has never been looked at since, that is the roof worth checking, whether or not you can see anything from the driveway.

What does roof repair in McKinney usually involve?

Most McKinney repairs come down to shingles, flashing, or sealing, not the whole roof. Replacing a handful of shingles the wind lifted, resealing flashing that dried out in our heat, securing a loose ridge cap, or tracing and fixing a single leak are the everyday jobs.

McKinney runs a wider spread of roof ages than most North Texas suburbs, and that is the real story here. Census American Community Survey estimates put the median McKinney home's construction year at around 2007, close behind Frisco, but McKinney also carries a small older core that Frisco does not: about 2% of the city's housing was built before 1950. Around the historic downtown square there are homes from that era with steep pitches, cut-up rooflines, and brick chimneys whose flashing has been sealed over more than once instead of replaced. Out west in Stonebridge Ranch and Craig Ranch the housing matches the citywide pattern of 2000s and 2010s construction, and the problem there is builder-grade shingle wearing out on schedule rather than a roof that has run out of years. The steep old roofs near the square also need a different approach on the day, because you cannot walk them the way you walk a 2000s hip roof. The weather does not care which one you have. Our summers bake shingles for months, our springs bring hail, and both go after the weak spots first. A flashing joint that cracked in the sun is a small fix today and a soaked attic after the next hard rain. We would rather patch the weak spot in calm weather than meet you after the water is already inside.

If your home sits inside McKinney's historic overlay near downtown, there is one extra step worth knowing about: the city's Historic Preservation Office allows straight in-kind roof repairs and replacements, matching material for material, without a separate historic review in most cases, though the city has the final word on any specific address. It is only when you want to change the roofing material or the look of the roofline that the city requires a Certificate of Appropriateness first. We will tell you upfront which category your job falls into so there are no surprises with the city partway through the work.
