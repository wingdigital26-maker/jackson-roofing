What does roof repair in Rockwall usually involve?

Shingles, flashing, or sealing. Very rarely the whole roof.

The everyday jobs are replacing a handful of shingles the wind took, resealing flashing that our summers dried out, securing a ridge cap that lifted, or tracing a leak back to its actual source, which is usually several feet uphill from where the ceiling stain shows up.

Rockwall sits on the east side of the metroplex above Lake Ray Hubbard, and the lake is the local variable. Wind coming across open water reaches the ridgelines around Chandlers Landing and the neighborhoods above the shoreline with nothing in the way to slow it down, and the newer lots out toward Stone Creek have far less mature tree cover than the older Dallas suburbs. Wind gets a clean run at roofs here, which is why ridge caps and the first course above an eave are the two things we check before anything else in this city. Combine that with attic temperatures past 140 degrees in July and August, which drives the oils out of asphalt shingles and leaves them brittle, and the weak spots give first. A shingle that lost its granules to two Texas summers is a small fix today and a soaked attic after the next hard rain.

What are the signs my Rockwall roof needs repair?

The useful signs are visible from the driveway: missing or curling shingles, dark bald patches, grit collecting at the downspout, or a brown ring on an interior ceiling.

Here is what we tell Rockwall homeowners to watch for:

  • Shingles missing, cracked, or curling. Visible from the street and an open door for water.
  • Granules collecting where the downspout empties. A shingle sheds its protective grit as it ages, and a lot of grit means the surface is wearing thin.
  • Flashing that looks loose, lifted, or rusted. The metal around chimneys, vents, and valleys is where most leaks actually start, not the open field of the roof.
  • A ceiling or attic stain. Even a small brown ring means water is getting past the roof somewhere, and it gets worse with every storm.
  • A lifted or cracked ridge cap. The shingles along the peak take the most wind, so they loosen first.
  • Shingle fragments in the yard after a storm. Something let go up there, and it is worth knowing what.

Any one of those is worth a look. Two or more together and it is worth a look this week.

Can my roof be repaired instead of replaced?

Often, yes, and we will tell you honestly when it can.

A roof under fifteen years old with an isolated problem, a leak in one valley or a patch of storm-struck shingles, is almost always a repair. What moves a roof into the replacement column is spread: granule loss across three or more slopes, leaks in unrelated places at once, decking that has gone soft, or the same spot failing for the third time.

We do not push a new roof on someone who needs a five shingle fix. About half our inspections end with us saying the roof is fine, which is a terrible sales strategy, and we do it anyway. When a roof genuinely is finished we say that too, and show you the photos so you can see why. Our roof replacement page covers that side of the decision in full.
