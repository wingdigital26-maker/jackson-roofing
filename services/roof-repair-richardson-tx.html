What does roof repair in Richardson usually involve?

Shingles, flashing, or sealant. Very rarely the whole roof.

The everyday jobs are replacing shingles the wind took, resealing flashing our summers dried out, securing a ridge cap that lifted, or tracing a leak back to where it actually starts, which is usually several feet uphill from wherever the ceiling stain appeared.

Richardson has a lot of housing stock from the 1960s through the 1980s, which matters more than people expect. Canyon Creek, Richardson Heights, and the streets around Prairie Creek are full of low pitched ranch rooflines from that era, and a low pitch is less forgiving than a steep one. Water leaves it slowly, so tired flashing and a split pipe boot show up as a stain on your ceiling far sooner than they would on a modern steep hip roof. Those roofs have often been done twice already, and the second layer, the older decking underneath, and whatever shortcut a previous crew took are frequently the real story. We find a fair amount of flashing on Richardson homes that was sealed rather than replaced the last time around, and sealant does not last through twenty North Texas summers.

Add attic temperatures past 140 degrees from May through September, which drive the oils out of asphalt and leave shingles brittle, and the weak points give first. This page is the Richardson version of our roof repair service.

Do you need a permit for roof repair in Richardson?

Sometimes, and the threshold is specific. The City of Richardson requires a permit for roof replacement, recovering, or repair that exceeds 25 percent of the total roof area.

In practice that means a normal repair, a few shingles or one section of flashing, does not need one. A large repair spanning more than a quarter of the roof does, and so does any full replacement. We pull the permit as part of the job rather than leaving it with you, and it is worth knowing the rule exists, because a crew that offers to skip it is telling you something about how they work generally.

That threshold is published by the City of Richardson, and we check it against the scope of the work before we quote. When a permit does apply, the city requires an inspection of the roof deck itself where decking is exposed, on top of the final inspection once the new covering is on, so a crew cannot bury bad decking under new shingles and call it done.

Source: City of Richardson, Building Inspection, Roof Repair / Replacement permit page (cor.net).
