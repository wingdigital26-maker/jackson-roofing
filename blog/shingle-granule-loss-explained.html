Granule Loss: What Those Grains Actually Mean

By Chris Jackson, Owner · September 13, 2026

The first time most people notice granule loss, they are not on the roof at all. They are cleaning out a downspout, or sweeping the driveway after a storm, and there is a small pile of what looks like coarse black sand at the bottom.

Those grains came off your shingles. That much is certain. What is far less certain, and what nobody seems to explain properly, is whether that pile means your roof is failing or whether it means almost nothing at all.

I have been pulling shingles off North Texas roofs since 2000, and granule loss is probably the single most misread symptom in this trade. It gets used to sell roofs that did not need selling. It also gets waved off on roofs that genuinely were finished. Here is the honest version.

What are roof granules and what do they do?

The coloured grains on the surface of an asphalt shingle are crushed mineral, usually a ceramic coated rock, pressed into the asphalt while it is still hot.

They are not decoration, although they are the reason your roof is grey or brown instead of tar black. Their real job is shade. Asphalt breaks down in ultraviolet light, and the granule layer is the sunscreen sitting between the sun and the asphalt underneath. Strip that layer away and the asphalt starts oxidising directly, which is a one way process.

The second job is armour. A granule surface takes the impact of wind driven grit and rain and spreads it out. It is also what gives a roof much of its fire rating and a good part of its traction.

So the layer matters. What matters just as much is how much of it is actually gone, and that is where most of the panic comes from.

Is some granule loss normal?

Yes, and quite a lot of it is.

Brand new shingles shed. There are always loose granules sitting on the surface that never bonded properly at the factory, and the first few rains wash them straight into the gutters. Homeowners call us about this every single year, usually within a month of a new roof going on, convinced something went wrong. Nothing did. That shedding tapers off within the first season.

After that, a roof keeps releasing granules slowly for its whole life. A cup of grit collected over a year on a fifteen year old roof is the roof aging at the speed it is supposed to. That is not a problem to solve.

What matters is the pattern, not the presence. Even, gradual thinning across the whole roof is wear. Concentrated loss, sudden loss, or bare patches are something else.

When granule loss is actually a problem

Here is what changes the answer from wear to damage.

  • Black spots the size of a coin, scattered randomly. This is the pattern hail leaves. The stone knocks granules off at the point of impact and bruises the mat underneath. Random placement across slopes is the giveaway.
  • Loss concentrated below one valley or one downspout outlet. Water running the same path every storm scours a channel. It usually means the drainage above it is doing something it should not.
  • Bare asphalt you can actually see. Not thin, bare. A shiny or dull black surface with no grain left is a shingle with its sunscreen gone, and it will age fast from there.
  • A sudden pile after one storm. A season of grit is normal. A handful of grit the morning after a hailstorm is a different conversation.
  • Curled or cupped edges alongside the loss. When the shingle is also deforming, the mat has dried out. The granules leaving are a symptom of something further along.

That third one is the line I use on roofs. Thinning is age. Bare is damage.

What hail does to granules on a North Texas roof

This is not a hypothetical here. The NOAA Storm Events Database logged 137 hail reports across Collin, Denton, Dallas, Rockwall and Kaufman counties between June 2024 and May 2026, on 27 separate storm days, and 122 of those reports involved stones an inch across or larger.

Some of them were well past that. A spotter report from 25 March 2025 recorded a 2.25 inch stone near Parker. A photo posted on 25 April 2026 showed hail estimated at up to 3 inches around Dallas Baptist University. On 25 September 2024 two inch hail was reported at Parker Road and Midway in Plano, and again near the Dallas North Tollway and Texas 121 in Frisco the same day.

A stone that size does not need to break anything to do damage. It knocks the granule layer off at the impact point and leaves the asphalt below exposed and often bruised. From the ground the roof looks completely fine. From four feet away you can see a field of dark circles where the armour used to be. We walk through the wider set of symptoms in the signs of hail damage on a roof.

Those hail dates cluster hard. Thirty seven of those reports came in March and thirty four in April, with another twenty four in September. If you want to know when to look, look after those months.

How to check your own roof without climbing on it

Stay on the ground. A wet composition roof is genuinely dangerous and we would rather you never got on one.

Open the downspout outlets and look at what has collected. Sweep the patio and the driveway after a storm and see whether there is grit there that was not there yesterday. Then stand at the corners of the house and look across each slope at a low angle rather than straight on, because raking light shows texture that a face on view flattens out. A slope that looks patchy or mottled where the neighbouring slope looks uniform is worth a closer look.

Look at the edges of the shingles too. Curling, cupping and lifted corners tend to travel with real granule loss, and together they tell you more than either does alone. Our roof maintenance checklist for Texas homeowners covers the rest of the ground level walk.

Does granule loss mean I need a new roof?

Often no, and I would rather say that plainly than leave it hanging.

A roof with even thinning and years of service life left is a roof you keep. A roof with scattered bare spots from a hailstorm may only need the affected slopes addressed, which is repair work rather than a replacement. A roof where the granule layer has genuinely gone and the mat underneath is brittle and curling is a roof that is done, and no repair changes that.

The only way to tell which of those three you own is for somebody to get up there and look at the shingle surface properly. That is what we do, we photograph it, and we show you the pictures. Frequently asked questions

Is it normal to find granules in the gutter?

Yes. Every asphalt roof sheds granules slowly for its whole life, and a brand new roof sheds heavily for the first season as loose factory grains wash off. A steady trickle is age. A sudden pile after one storm is worth checking.

Can granules grow back or be replaced?

No. Granules are pressed into the asphalt at the factory while it is hot, and there is no way to reattach them on an installed roof. Anything sold as a granule restoration coating is covering the problem, not fixing it.

How can I tell hail granule loss from normal wear?

Pattern. Normal wear thins evenly across whole slopes. Hail leaves scattered round spots of bare asphalt in no particular order, often with a soft bruise in the mat underneath that a roofer can feel with a thumb.

How long does a shingle last once the granules are gone?

Once real areas of asphalt are exposed, that part of the roof ages quickly, because the layer holding ultraviolet light off it is no longer there. It is not a countdown anyone can put a number on, which is exactly why it is worth looking rather than guessing.
