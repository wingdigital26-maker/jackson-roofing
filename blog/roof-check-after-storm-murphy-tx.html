After a Storm in Murphy TX: What to Check on Your Roof

By Chris Jackson, Owner · September 28, 2026

The morning after hail in Murphy, most people do the same thing: step into the yard, look up, and try to decide whether the roof is fine. From the ground you can learn more than you might think, as long as you know where to look and you stay off the ladder.

What the hail record shows for Murphy

Murphy sits in Collin County between Plano and Wylie, and it has taken real hail in the last two seasons. On May 6, 2025, public reports through mPING logged 1.25 inch hail, about half dollar size, and 1.5 inch hail, about ping pong ball size, in Murphy on the same day. On April 27, 2026, two more reports came in at 1 inch, roughly quarter size. There was also a smaller 0.75 inch report just east of town on March 8, 2025.

Those numbers matter because size changes what hail does to a roof. Small stones mostly knock granules loose. Once you get to an inch and above, asphalt shingles can bruise and crack under the granule layer, even when nothing looks broken from the street. If your home was in Murphy for either of those spring storms and nobody has looked at the roof since, it is worth a check. Homes a few miles east saw a similar pattern, and our post on the hail record for Wylie roofs walks through those dates.

Start on the ground, and stay there

Do not climb onto the roof to look. Wet shingles are slick, bruised shingles can tear under a boot, and walking a damaged roof can make the damage worse. Everything in this post can be done from the yard, the driveway, or across the street.

Walk the whole house slowly. Stand far enough back that you see each roof plane at an angle rather than straight on. Low morning or evening light across the shingles shows dents and scuffs better than midday sun. If you own binoculars, bring them. Take photos on your phone as you go so you have a dated record of what the roof looked like right after the storm.

The clues that usually show up first

Soft metal gives hail away before shingles do. Check these on your way around the house:

  • Roof vents and caps: dents in the metal vents, ridge caps or pipe collars are the clearest sign the stones were big enough to matter.
  • Gutters and downspouts: fresh dings, plus a drift of dark granules washing out of the downspout onto the driveway.
  • Screens, AC fins and the mailbox: spatter marks and dents on the side the storm came from.
  • Shingles: dark spots where granules are gone, torn or missing tabs, and edges the wind has lifted.
  • Siding and fence: fresh chips or dents, usually heaviest on one side of the house.

A few granules in the gutter after any storm is normal wear. A sudden heavy drift after hail is different, and our guide to what shingle granule loss means explains how to tell the two apart.

Which side of the house to check hardest

Hail rarely falls straight down. Wind drives it at an angle, so one or two faces of the roof take most of the hits while the others look clean. Notice which side of your fence, siding or car got marked, then spend most of your time on the roof planes facing that way. A roof that looks fine from the front yard can be badly bruised on the back slope. For more of the visual clues we look for on every job, see hail damage roof signs you can spot from the ground.

Do not forget the flashing and roof edges

Flashing is the metal that seals the joints where the roof meets a wall, a chimney or a vent pipe. Hail can dent it and wind can lift it, and either one gives water a path behind the shingles. From the ground, look for flashing that looks bent, pulled away, or out of line with the rest of the roof. Along the eaves, look for drip edge that has been knocked crooked. Our post on where roof flashing leaks start shows the spots that fail most often.

Inside, check the ceilings on the top floor. If you can get into the attic safely, take a flashlight up after the next rain. Fresh water stains, damp insulation or daylight through the decking are all worth a call.

A walkaround tells you whether something is worth checking. It cannot tell you whether a shingle is bruised underneath, whether the seal strips let go, or whether water has started working under the decking. Those need someone on the roof who knows what hail damage looks and feels like up close.

Frequently asked questions

How soon after hail should I check my roof?

Within a day or two, from the ground. Damage that looks minor can turn into a leak with the next rain, and fresh dents on vents and gutters are easier to spot before they weather.

Is 1 inch hail big enough to damage a roof?

It can be. Around an inch is where hail starts bruising and cracking asphalt shingles instead of only knocking granules loose, especially on older roofs. Murphy logged 1 inch reports on April 27, 2026 and reports up to 1.5 inch on May 6, 2025.

Should I get on the roof to check for damage?

No. Wet or damaged shingles are slippery and can tear, and walking on them can make the damage worse. Look from the yard and let a roofer handle anything closer.
