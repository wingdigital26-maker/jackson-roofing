Metal Roof vs Shingles in Texas: Cost and What to Know

By Chris Jackson, Owner · August 20, 2026

Metal roofs cost more upfront and last longer. Asphalt shingles cost less to install, are easier to repair, and still perform well in North Texas if you choose the right grade. The honest answer for most homeowners: pick the one that fits your budget and how long you plan to stay in the house.

We have been installing both on North Texas homes since 2000, and the conversation depends almost entirely on three things: your budget today, how long you intend to own the house, and how much hail your area has taken in recent years.

What does each option actually cost in Texas?

Asphalt shingles: priced per square foot installed, and the number moves with the grade you pick. Three-tab is the cheapest. Architectural shingles cost more and hold up better in heat and hail. Class 4 impact-resistant shingles sit at the top of the asphalt range.

Metal roofing: priced the same way, but higher. Panel style is the biggest driver. Corrugated is the lower end, standing seam the higher end, and steel gauge moves the number again inside each style.

As a rule of thumb, metal lands two to three times the cost of asphalt shingles on the same house. That gap is real. The question is whether the longer lifespan and lower maintenance justify it for your specific situation, and that is a number we put in writing rather than guess at over the phone.

Which holds up better in North Texas heat and hail?

Texas summers push attic temps above 140 degrees. Spring hail seasons in Collin County can produce quarter-size to golf-ball-size hailstones. Both materials have to handle those conditions, and how you maintain them matters too, which our roof maintenance checklist for Texas homeowners covers.

Metal: Stands up to heat well. For hail, the outcome depends on gauge and panel style. Thin corrugated panels can dent under large hail. Thicker standing-seam steel handles it better and holds its waterproof seams through impact.

Asphalt shingles: Standard three-tab shingles are vulnerable to large hail. Class 4 impact-resistant architectural shingles are a significant upgrade and are worth the cost in hail-prone Collin County. They carry an impact-resistance rating from Underwriters Laboratories and last meaningfully longer after a bad storm season.

If your main concern is hail, Class 4 shingles are often the practical choice: better protection than standard shingles at a fraction of metal's upfront cost.

Which is easier to repair?

Shingles win here. If a section of asphalt shingles lifts, cracks, or loses granules, we match and replace the affected area as a roof repair without touching the rest of the roof. Parts are readily available.

Metal panels are trickier. Matching an existing panel after a dent or puncture can be difficult if the original run is no longer manufactured. Some repairs end up visible as a patch. For homeowners who keep their house long-term, metal's low-maintenance profile outweighs this. For homeowners who prioritize easy repair access, shingles are simpler.

Is a metal roof worth it in North Texas?

For most homeowners replacing a 20- to 25-year-old shingle roof: not automatically. The math depends on how long you plan to be in the house. If you are staying 30 or more years, metal can pay for itself in avoided replacements. If you are selling in the next decade, you likely will not recoup the premium.

If your roof is already near the end of its life, the more useful comparison is not metal against shingles, it is repair against replacement. We walk through the wear patterns that decide it in Texas heat and roof damage.

FAQ

How much more does a metal roof cost than shingles in Texas?

Metal typically costs two to three times more upfront. The gap closes over time because metal lasts significantly longer. Call (469) 323-4626 for a written quote on both options for your home.

Does a metal roof hold up better to Texas hail?

Thicker standing-seam steel handles hail well. Class 4 impact-resistant shingles are also a strong hail option at a lower upfront cost than most metal systems.

How long does a metal roof last in Texas?

A properly installed metal roof lasts 40 to 70 years depending on panel type and coating. Asphalt shingles average 20 to 25 years in North Texas conditions.

Which is easier to repair, metal or shingles?

Shingles are easier and cheaper to repair. Metal panels can be harder to match and repair consistently.
