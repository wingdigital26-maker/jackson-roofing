How to Choose a Roofer in Plano Without Getting Burned

By Chris Jackson, Owner · August 31, 2026

Choose a Plano roofer with a real local address you can drive to, a crew they can name, and a written scope that lists the shingle line, the underlayment, the flashing work, and what happens if the decking underneath turns out to be rotten. Get two or three quotes. Walk away from anyone who needs your signature today.

That is the short version. Here is what it actually looks like when you are standing in your driveway holding two estimates that do not match.

Why Plano fills up with out-of-town crews after a storm

Plano sits in the middle of the North Texas hail corridor. Every time a serious cell moves across Collin County, crews from outside the area follow it in. They knock doors hard for two or three weeks, sign as many contracts as they can, and are gone before the first callback comes in. Whatever they leave on your roof becomes your problem, not theirs. We handle storm and hail damage here year round, long after those crews have moved on.

A knock on the door is not automatically a scam. It does mean the burden of proof sits with them. A roofer who actually works here will clear that bar without getting defensive about it, because they have already answered these questions a hundred times.

Plano roofs are not all the same age

This is the part that gets missed. Plano is not one housing stock, it is several. The older neighborhoods on the east side, the big buildout through west Plano around Legacy Drive and Willow Bend, and the newer construction up near Legacy West are all sitting at very different points in a roof's life.

That matters because the right answer changes with the roof. On a roof that still has years left, chasing a small leak is usually the correct call and a full replacement is not. On a roof already past its window, patching one slope while three others are failing is money you will spend twice. A roofer who quotes the same job on every house on the street is not looking closely enough at yours. Our roof maintenance checklist for Texas homeowners covers what to look at on your own roof first.

Five checks worth doing before you sign

  1. A physical address you can drive to. Not a P.O. box, not a service area. If something goes wrong in year three, you want a door to knock on.
  2. Know who is on the roof. Ask whether the crew works for the company or whether the job gets sold and handed off. Either answer can be fine. A company that cannot tell you has told you something.
  3. Scope in writing, down to the flashing. A one line bid that says replace roof is how surprise charges happen. The written scope should name the shingle line, the underlayment, the drip edge, the flashing work, and the price if the deck underneath is rotten.
  4. Recent local references. Anyone can produce three happy customers from six years ago. Ask for Plano or Collin County addresses from the last twelve months that you can drive past.
  5. No pressure on the timeline. Storm damage does not expire in forty eight hours. A roofer pushing you to sign today is protecting their schedule, not your roof.

Questions that tell you who you are actually hiring

Take these into any bid meeting and listen to how they get answered, not just what gets said:

  • How long have you worked in Plano specifically, and where do you work out of?
  • Who pulls the permit with the city, you or me?
  • What manufacturer certifications do you hold, and can I verify them on the manufacturer's website?
  • What material are you quoting for the job, and why that one? Our metal roof versus shingles comparison is a useful check on the answer.
  • What does the warranty actually cover, and is it on the material, the workmanship, or both?
  • What happens if you open the roof up and find rot underneath?

A roofer who answers these plainly is usually the one worth hiring. A roofer who gets vague or changes the subject is telling you something too.

Comparing two quotes that do not look alike

Two written estimates rarely line up, and that makes comparing them harder than it should be. Look past the number at the bottom and check that both quotes cover the same work: the shingle brand and grade, whether the flashing and vents are being replaced or reused, the underlayment type, and whether tear off and disposal are included.

A lower number that skips steps the other quote includes is not the better deal, it is a different job. If a quote leaves you with more questions than answers, ask before you sign. A straightforward roofer will walk you through the difference line by line without getting touchy about it.

The bottom line

A good roofer explains, an unreliable one rushes. Jackson Roofing has worked out of Plano since 2000, which is not something a crew that showed up after the last storm can say.
