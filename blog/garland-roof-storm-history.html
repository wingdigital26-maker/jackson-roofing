Garland Roofs and the Storms That Age Them

By Chris Jackson, Owner · September 22, 2026

Garland was largely built out before the Collin County suburbs went up, which means a lot of its roofs have already sat through more storm seasons than the ones a few exits north. That history matters more than any single hailstorm.

What does the hail record actually show for Garland?

The National Weather Service keeps a public log of severe weather reports, and Garland appears in it by name. On 2025-05-06 a trained spotter reported 1 inch hail in Garland. The same day a social media picture showed half dollar sized hail, logged at 1.25 inches, in North Garland, and a third report came in through mPING at 1 inch. Three separate reports, one afternoon, one city.

That is the honest scale of it. Garland has not been hit by the headline stones. The largest hail logged anywhere in Dallas County in the last two years was 3.0 inches near Lakeview on 2026-04-25, where a photo showed stones estimated up to three inches across and car windows blown out. Garland's own reports sit at the quarter to half dollar end. A homeowner reading national storm coverage could easily assume nothing happened here at all.

Across the five counties we work in, the record holds 137 hail reports in 24 months, 122 of them an inch or larger, spread over 27 distinct storm days. Roofs in this part of Texas are not waiting for one catastrophic event. They are absorbing a lot of medium-sized hits.

Why does a one inch stone matter on an older roof?

A one inch stone on a roof laid last spring usually does very little. The asphalt is still flexible, the granules are packed tight, and the mat underneath has plenty of oil left in it. The stone bounces and the roof shrugs.

The same stone on a roof in its fifteenth Texas summer behaves differently. Years of heat drive the volatile oils out of the asphalt, the mat gets brittle, and granules loosen. Now the stone does not bounce so much as dent, and a dent in a brittle mat is a fracture that opens over the following months. This is why two houses on the same Garland street can take identical hail and get completely different outcomes. If you want the longer version of the heat side of that story, we wrote up why Texas heat ages roofs faster.

Age is the multiplier. The storm is only the trigger.

What does repeated hail do over the years?

Damage from repeated moderate hail rarely announces itself. It accumulates. A few bruises one spring, a few more two seasons later, and then a spring where the roof starts letting water past in a spot that looks no different from the rest of it.

The pattern we see most on older Garland roofs looks like this:

  • Granule loss in a scattered pattern rather than the even wear of ordinary aging, with grit collecting at the downspout outlets.
  • Soft spots underfoot where the mat has fractured beneath an intact-looking surface.
  • Shiny black circles where the granules have been knocked clear and the asphalt is exposed to direct sun.
  • Dented metal at the vents and flashing, which is often the easiest evidence to see from the ground.
  • Sealant strips that have let go on the slope that faces the prevailing storm direction.

Can you tell from the ground?

Some of it, yes, and you should look. Stand back in the yard on a clear morning and use the sun at a low angle, because raking light shows texture that midday sun flattens out. Look at the soft metal first. Vents, flashing and the metal edge trim dent at a lower threshold than shingles do, so they are the honest witness on the roof.

Then look at the gutters and the splash blocks for loose granules. A handful after a new roof sheds its excess is normal. A steady accumulation years later is not. We put the full ground level checklist in our writeup of hail damage roof signs you can spot from the ground.

What you cannot do from the yard is judge the mat. Bruising is felt, not seen, and that means someone has to be on the roof to know. Please do not make that someone yourself. A Texas roof in August is hot enough to scuff under your shoes, and scuffing an aging roof causes the exact damage you were checking for.

Repair or replace an older Garland roof?

The question is not how much damage there is. It is whether the roof has enough life left to be worth repairing. A twelve year old roof with damage in one plane is usually a straightforward roof repair. A twenty two year old roof with scattered bruising across three slopes is a different conversation, because patching it buys a season and then the next slope fails.

Neighboring cities give a useful comparison here. We see the same age-versus-damage math on the older streets in the roofs we look at in Richardson, where the housing stock runs close to Garland's in age and the 2026-04-27 storm put 1.75 inch hail on the ground there, measured and relayed by a member of the public.

An honest answer needs someone to look at the actual roof. There is no responsible way to decide this from a satellite photo or a knock at the door.

What about the roofers who show up after a storm?

Garland gets the same post-storm traffic every other North Texas city gets. Crews follow the radar, work a neighborhood for a few weeks and move on. Some are competent. Some will be unreachable by the time a repair needs standing behind.

The check that matters is simple: can you find this company a year from now, in this area, at a fixed address. Frequently asked questions

How old is too old for a Garland roof?

There is no single number. A well ventilated roof in good shade can outlast a poorly ventilated one on a south facing slope by years. What matters more than the date on the paperwork is how much the asphalt has hardened, and that is something you can feel on the roof but not see from the street.

Does hail this size really damage a roof?

One inch hail can absolutely damage an aging roof, and often will not touch a new one. The reports filed under Garland on 2025-05-06 were 1.0 to 1.25 inches. That is squarely in the range where the condition of the roof decides the outcome.

Should I wait until it leaks?

Waiting turns a shingle question into a decking and interior question. Water that gets past a fractured mat goes into the boards first, and replacing wet decking costs more than dealing with the shingles would have.

How long does an inspection take?

Looking at a typical Garland home takes under an hour. Most of that is on the roof itself, checking the soft metal, feeling for bruising and photographing anything worth showing you afterward.
