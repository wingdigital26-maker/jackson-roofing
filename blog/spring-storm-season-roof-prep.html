When North Texas Hail Season Actually Peaks

By Chris Jackson, Owner · September 13, 2026

Ask around North Texas and everybody agrees hail season is spring. That is broadly right, and it is also vague enough to be useless. Spring is three months long, and by the time most people start thinking about their roof, the worst of it has usually already come through.

So rather than repeat the folklore, we went and counted. The NOAA Storm Events Database publishes every hail report logged by spotters, and that record tells you fairly precisely when hail actually falls here, which months it skips entirely, and where the second peak nobody talks about sits.

What the hail record actually shows

Across Collin, Denton, Dallas, Rockwall and Kaufman counties, NOAA logged 137 hail reports between June 2024 and May 2026. Those reports fell on just 27 separate storm days, and 122 of them involved stones an inch across or larger.

That first number is worth sitting with. Twenty seven days in two years. Hail here is not a constant drizzle of small events, it is a handful of days that matter enormously and a great many days that do not. A single one of those 27 days can put a new roof on half a neighbourhood.

The month by month spread of those reports looks like this:

  • March, 37 reports. The heaviest month in the record.
  • April, 34 reports. Close behind, and the month that produced the largest stones.
  • September, 24 reports. The peak almost nobody prepares for.
  • May, 22 reports. Still a serious month.
  • June, 12 reports. The tail end of the spring pattern.
  • November and December, 8 reports between them. Uncommon, but not zero.

The months that do not appear at all are just as informative. In that two year window there was not a single hail report logged in January, February, July, August or October across those five counties.

So when does hail season really start?

March, and it is already running by the time most people notice.

The practical implication is that if you wait for the first storm on the news to think about your roof, you are behind. The useful window for getting a roof looked at is February, while nothing is happening, roofers are not buried, and there is time to actually schedule work rather than join a queue behind an entire damaged suburb.

March through May carries 93 of the 137 reports in the record. That is the bulk of the risk packed into three months.

The September peak nobody plans for

This is the part that surprises homeowners, and it is the part worth changing your habits over.

September produced 24 reports in the record, more than May did. On 21 September 2025 a spotter logged two inch hail near Melissa. The year before, on 25 September 2024, two inch hail was reported at Parker Road and Midway in Plano, and again near the Dallas North Tollway and Texas 121 in Frisco on the same day.

Nobody has their roof on their mind in September. The spring scare has faded, the summer has been dry, and hail is not what anyone is watching for. That is exactly why September damage sits unnoticed on roofs through the autumn and only announces itself as a leak in the winter rain.

If you take one thing from the record, make it this: check the roof in late August as well as in February.

How big does it actually get here?

Bigger than the pea sized hail people picture when they shrug a storm off.

The largest report in the record is 3 inch hail, from a photo posted on 25 April 2026 around Dallas Baptist University. Two days later, on 27 April 2026, a report from Fate described hail large enough to leave holes in metal. On 25 March 2025 a 2.25 inch stone was photographed near Parker.

For scale, a one inch stone is about the size of a quarter, and 122 of the 137 reports were at least that. A stone that size falling at speed will strip granules off a shingle and bruise the mat underneath without necessarily cracking anything visible from the street. What it does to aged flashing and rubber pipe boots is worse, and that is covered in the five places a roof usually leaks first.

What to actually do before the season

None of this requires getting on the roof, and we would rather you did not.

What is genuinely useful before a season is knowing the condition your roof is starting from. After a storm, the question everybody struggles with is whether the damage is new. If somebody has already photographed your roof in February, that question has an answer.

The rest is simple ground level work. Walk the perimeter and look for anything already lifted, curled or missing. Check the yard and the driveway for shingle grit, which is covered properly in what granule loss actually means. Clear the drainage lines so water leaves the roof instead of sitting on it. Trim back any limb that is close enough to whip against shingles in a straight line wind, because wind does more roof damage in these storms than people credit.

Then leave it alone. A sound roof needs preparation, not intervention.

After a storm, slow down

The day after a big hail day, the knocking starts. That is worth expecting rather than reacting to.

A storm that produces 30 reports in one afternoon draws crews from several states, and some of them will be gone by the time anything goes wrong with the work. There is no urgency that justifies signing something on your doorstep. A roof that got hit yesterday will be in the same condition next week, and any real damage will still be there for a second opinion to find. Frequently asked questions

When is hail season in North Texas?

March through May carries most of it. In the NOAA record for Collin, Denton, Dallas, Rockwall and Kaufman counties, those three months account for 93 of 137 hail reports, with March the heaviest single month.

Does it hail here in the autumn?

Yes, and more than most people expect. September produced 24 reports in that record, more than May, including two inch hail near Melissa in September 2025 and across Plano and Frisco in September 2024.

Which months never see hail here?

Across that two year window, no hail reports at all were logged in January, February, July, August or October in those five counties. That makes February a sensible month to have a roof looked at.

Should I have my roof inspected before or after a storm?

Both is ideal, and before matters more than people think. A photographed record of the roof beforehand is what lets anyone tell new damage from old wear afterwards.
