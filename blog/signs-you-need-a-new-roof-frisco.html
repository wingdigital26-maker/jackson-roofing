7 Signs Your Frisco Home Needs a New Roof

By Chris Jackson, Owner · October 2, 2026

Curling or cracked shingles, bald spots where the granules have worn off, grit collecting under the downspouts, stains on an interior ceiling, and daylight showing through the attic are the signs that matter most. Any two of those together on a roof past fifteen years usually means it is time for a real look rather than another patch.

You can spot most of this from the driveway in about ten minutes, without climbing anything. Here is what to look for on a Frisco home, and what each sign actually means.

Why so many Frisco roofs are hitting this window at once

Frisco grew faster than almost anywhere in North Texas, and a large share of its homes went up in a concentrated stretch of building through the 2000s and after. A standard asphalt shingle roof here lasts roughly fifteen to twenty years, so whole subdivisions built within a few years of each other now reach the end of that window within a few years of each other too.

That is why you see three or four homes on the same street getting work done in the same season. It is not always storm damage. Often it is just the original builder roof finally aging out on a street where every roof went on at the same time. If your neighbors are replacing and your house went up in the same build, that is worth a look even if nothing seems wrong from the ground. Our post on what a full Frisco reroof involves walks through the job itself if you end up there.

What you can see from the driveway

1. Curling or cracked shingles. Shingles that cup upward at the edges, or show cracks when you look closely, are past their prime. Texas heat speeds this along, since UV exposure and constant temperature swings dry the asphalt out faster than a milder climate would. Once shingles curl they stop shedding water the way they were designed to, and wind gets under them more easily.

2. Bald spots where the granules have worn off. Those granules are not decoration. They shield the asphalt underneath from the sun. Smoother, darker or shinier patches on the roof mean the protective layer is thinning in that spot, and once the asphalt is exposed it ages quickly. Our guide to why shingles shed their granules covers what is normal and what is not.

3. Grit collecting under the downspouts. After a hard rain, look at the ground where the downspout empties and along the roof edge. A build up of gritty, sand like debris means the shingles are shedding their coating faster than normal. A handful in the first months after a new roof goes on is expected. A steady pile on a fifteen year old roof is not.

What you find inside, or after a storm

4. Dark stains on a ceiling. A ceiling stain almost always means water is getting in somewhere above it. It does not have to be dramatic. A light brown ring in the corner of a room is enough to warrant a look, and a slow leak like that can work through insulation and framing long before it becomes obvious. Stains near a chimney, a wall or a vent often point to flashing rather than the shingles themselves.

5. Daylight showing through the attic. If you can get into your attic safely, look up at the roof deck for pinpricks of light coming through. This is the clearest sign on the list, because it means there is an actual gap in the roofing system, not just wear. Soft or sagging spots in the deck, seen from below, belong in the same category.

6. Damage you cannot see from the ground after hail. Hail bruising often does not show up from the driveway at all. It knocks granules loose and shortens the roof's remaining life without leaving an obvious hole. Frisco is not spared. On September 25, 2024, the NOAA storm record logged hail across Frisco, from nickel and quarter size reports up to two inch hail near the Dallas North Tollway and Texas 121, and another inch hail report came in southeast of Frisco on March 29, 2025. If your roof came through either of those, it is worth knowing how it fared. Our list of hail damage signs you can spot from the ground helps, but this is the one sign you genuinely cannot self diagnose, and it is the reason a free roof inspection exists.

When age tips the decision

7. Age plus symptoms. Age by itself is not a reason to replace a roof. A twenty year old roof that is holding up well is a twenty year old roof that is holding up well.

What changes the math is age plus symptoms. Once a roof passes about fifteen years in North Texas and starts showing two or three of the signs above at the same time, each new repair is worth judging against how much life the roof has left rather than fixing in isolation. Spending twice on a roof with three years left in it is the mistake we see most often. Attic heat plays into this as well, and a roof over a poorly vented attic tends to show these signs earlier than its neighbors.

What to do if you spot one of these

None of these signs automatically mean a full replacement. Plenty of them come back as a repair, especially when the damage is limited to one area and the rest of the roof is sound. If that is your situation, our page on getting a Frisco roof leak fixed covers how those visits run. The only way to know for sure is a proper look on the roof and in the attic, not a guess from the street and not a stranger's opinion at your front door.

Frequently asked questions

How many years does a roof last in Frisco?

A standard asphalt shingle roof here usually lasts roughly fifteen to twenty years. Heat, hail and attic ventilation all move that number, so condition matters more than the date alone.

Does one bad sign mean I need a whole new roof?

No. Plenty of single signs come back as a repair, especially when the damage sits in one area. It is two or three signs together on an older roof that usually points toward replacement.

Can I see hail damage from the ground?

Often not. Hail tends to bruise shingles and knock granules loose without leaving a hole, which is why a look on the roof itself is the only reliable check after a storm.
