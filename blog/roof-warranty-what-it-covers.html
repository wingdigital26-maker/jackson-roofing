Manufacturer vs Workmanship Roof Warranty

By Chris Jackson, Owner · September 29, 2026

A new roof in North Texas usually comes with two warranties, not one. One is written by the company that made the materials. The other is written by the crew that installed them. They protect different things, they last different lengths of time, and knowing which is which before you sign saves a lot of confusion later.

The manufacturer warranty covers the product

The manufacturer warranty is a promise about the materials themselves: the shingles, and sometimes the underlayment, starter strips and ridge caps when they come from the same maker. It says those products will hold up for a stated period without a manufacturing defect. Think shingles that crack, curl or blister early, or shed granules far faster than normal wear explains. If you want to know what normal looks like, our post on why shingles lose their granules walks through the difference between age and a bad batch.

What the manufacturer warranty does not do is judge how the roof went on. If a shingle fails because it was nailed in the wrong spot, that is not a product defect, and the manufacturer will say so. Many manufacturer warranties are also prorated, which means the share they will stand behind shrinks as the years go by. Read the length and the proration schedule as two separate numbers.

The workmanship warranty covers the installation

The workmanship warranty comes from your roofer. It covers the labor: nail placement, how the underlayment was laid, how the valleys were cut, and how the flashing was fitted around chimneys, walls and vents. When a roof leaks in its first few years, the cause is far more often an installation detail than a bad shingle. Flashing is the usual suspect, and our guide to the spots where flashing leaks start shows where to look.

This is the warranty that depends most on who you hire. A workmanship warranty is only worth something if the company that wrote it is still answering the phone when you need it. The length printed on the page matters less than whether the roofer has a track record in your area and a real address you can visit.

Where the two warranties leave gaps

Neither warranty is a promise that nothing will ever go wrong with the roof. Both are narrow on purpose. The manufacturer covers defects in the product. The roofer covers mistakes in the install. Everything else, including ordinary wear, a tree limb coming down, or storm impact on a roof that was built right, usually sits outside both.

Most warranties also carry conditions you have to keep up your end of. Common ones are keeping the attic properly ventilated, not letting another company make repairs without telling the installer, and not stacking new layers over old ones. Routine care helps you stay on the right side of those conditions, and our Texas roof maintenance checklist covers the simple seasonal checks worth keeping a record of.

Hail, and why a baseline record helps

Hail is the reason this matters so much here. Across the five counties we serve (Collin, Denton, Dallas, Rockwall and Kaufman), the NOAA storm record lists 137 hail reports between June 2024 and May 2026. Of those, 122 were one inch or larger, spread over 27 separate storm days. The biggest was 3 inch hail reported on April 25, 2026 near Lakeview in Dallas County, where the report described car windows blown out.

Storm impact is generally treated as outside a manufacturer warranty. Where it gets complicated is a roof that was already failing before a storm, or damage that is hard to tell apart from a defect. That is where a record of the roof's condition earns its keep. A written inspection soon after the install, with photos, gives you something to compare against later. Our page on what a roof inspection in Plano covers explains what that visit looks like. When a storm has already been through, our storm and hail damage repair page covers what happens next.

Questions to ask before you sign

  • Which products carry the manufacturer warranty? Ask whether it covers only the shingles or the whole set of components, and for how many years.
  • Is it prorated? Find out when the proration starts and what share is left in year ten or fifteen.
  • How long is the workmanship warranty, and what does it name? Leaks, flashing and ventilation should be spelled out, not implied.
  • Does either warranty transfer if you sell the house? Many do only with written notice inside a set window.
  • What do you have to do to keep it valid? Get the maintenance and registration conditions in writing.
  • Who do you contact, and what paperwork will they want? Keep the invoice, the product names and the install date together.

A roofer who is comfortable with these questions will answer them plainly and put the answers on paper. If the answers get vague, that tells you something about the warranty and about the company.

Frequently asked questions

Does a manufacturer warranty cover installation mistakes?

No. It covers defects in the materials. Problems caused by how the roof was installed fall under the workmanship warranty from the roofer who did the job.

Can a roof warranty be transferred to a new owner?

Often, but not automatically. Many warranties allow one transfer if the new owner registers it within a set time after the sale. Ask for the transfer terms in writing before you sign.

What if hail damages a roof that is still under warranty?

Storm impact is usually outside both warranties. If you believe a defect is involved, photos and an inspection report give you the best footing when you ask the manufacturer to honor the warranty.
