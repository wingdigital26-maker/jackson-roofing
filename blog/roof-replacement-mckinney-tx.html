Roof Replacement in McKinney TX: What to Expect

By Chris Jackson, Owner · September 14, 2026

Most people do not go looking for a roof replacement. It finds them, usually the morning after a storm, or the afternoon a real estate agent mentions the roof looks about done.

A roof replacement in McKinney means tearing the old roof off, checking and repairing the wood decking underneath, then installing new underlayment, flashing and shingles. On a typical home it is a one to two day job. The part nobody tells you is that the decision to replace at all is the expensive one to get wrong, in both directions.

I have been on North Texas roofs since 2000. What follows is how we actually think about a McKinney roof when we are standing on it.

When does a McKinney roof need replacing instead of repairing?

A roof needs replacing when it is at the end of its life, or when the damage runs across several sections rather than sitting in one spot. A roof past fifteen years with brittle, worn shingles in multiple areas is usually a replacement. A younger roof with one isolated problem is usually a repair.

We do not push a new roof on somebody who needs a patch. Plenty of McKinney homes are simply hitting the age where the original builder-grade roof has given what it had to give, and our summers do not slow that down. When we inspect, we show you the photographs so you can see the difference for yourself. If a repair will hold, roof repair is the first thing we look at, not the last.

What does the hail record actually show for McKinney?

This is worth being specific about, because "hail country" gets said so often it stops meaning anything.

On March 25, 2025 the National Weather Service logged half dollar sized hail, about an inch and a quarter, at Lake Forest and 380 in west McKinney. The same storm dropped ping pong ball sized hail, an inch and a half, at Hardin and Eldorado. Later that year, on November 24, 2025, numerous reports described small hail up to the size of nickels covering the ground in spots around McKinney, with dime sized hail out near Rustwood Acres to the west.

Notice how ordinary most of those sizes are. Homeowners tend to wait for the dramatic storm, the one that dents cars, before thinking about the roof. But an inch of hail arriving repeatedly over several seasons does quiet, cumulative work on a shingle surface, and that is the damage that goes unnoticed until it leaks. The signs are usually visible from the ground if you know what you are looking at, which we walk through in hail damage roof signs you can spot from the ground.

What drives the price of a roof replacement?

Four things account for nearly the whole number: the size and pitch of your roof, the material you choose, how many old layers have to come off, and the condition of the decking underneath.

That last one is why no honest roofer quotes a firm price sight unseen. Once the old roof is off we sometimes find soft or rotted plywood from a leak nobody knew about, and bad decking is replaced by the sheet. Two McKinney homes of similar size can land a long way apart for that reason alone. Anyone who gives you a final number over the phone, before measuring anything, is guessing, and the correction will arrive later. If you are weighing materials against long-term cost, we compared the two most common options in metal roof versus shingles in Texas.

What is the best roofing material for a McKinney home?

For most homes here, architectural asphalt shingles are the sensible pick. Impact-resistant shingles are worth a look given what the hail record above shows. Metal costs more up front and lasts longest if you plan to stay put for decades.

But the material matters less than most people assume, because our real problem is heat. McKinney summers push attic temperatures well past 140 degrees, and that bakes a roof from underneath no matter what is nailed on top. A roof that cannot breathe wears out early regardless of what you paid for the shingles, which is why we look at attic ventilation and roof lifespan on every replacement before we talk about product lines. Some McKinney neighborhoods also have rules on shingle color and style, and we confirm those before ordering material.

How long does the work take?

Most single-family homes are a one to two day job, weather permitting. Larger or steeper roofs take longer, and a rainy stretch will push the timeline, which is normal and a good deal safer than rushing a roof on under a storm.

We work in stages so your home is never left open to the weather overnight. If rain rolls in mid-job we make the roof watertight and finish when it clears. The plan and the timeline go into your written quote before anybody starts, so the schedule is not a surprise. There is more detail on the sequence in how long a roof replacement actually takes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my McKinney roof needs replacing?

A roof past fifteen years with worn shingles across several areas usually needs replacing. A younger roof with damage in one spot is usually a repair. We show you photographs so you can see which one you have.

Does hail that small really damage a roof?

It can, and the cumulative effect matters more than any single storm. Nickel and half dollar sized hail rarely looks dramatic from the ground, but repeated seasons of it wear the granule surface down and shorten the life of the roof.

Do you handle McKinney HOA shingle rules?

Yes. Several McKinney neighborhoods restrict shingle color and style. We confirm the rules before material is ordered so your new roof stays in good standing.
